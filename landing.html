How it works

Three things, every morning

1

Say it while it's still there

Dreams fade within minutes of waking. Open SomnuMatrix, type what you saw, and stop. No menus, no decisions, nothing to set up.

2

It builds what you said

The house with the red door. The woman who wouldn't speak. The road that sloped the wrong way. An AI reader goes through every word and builds only what you dreamt — the rest stays faint and unfinished, the way a half-remembered thing should.

3

Walk back in whenever you like

Your dreamscape keeps everything. Dream of that house again and it's still standing, with whatever you've since added. A year of dreams becomes a world of your own, with your own people, your own gods and roads out to other worlds.

"I was in a tall house with arched windows and a red door. My grandmother was in the kitchen. Outside, a black dog followed me down a road that sloped the wrong way. Then I was under the sea."

Every one of those becomes something you can stand in front of.

Why a map

Because memory is built for places

Dreams fade fast — most of a dream is gone within minutes of waking. But spatial memory is the strongest memory we have: it's why the old memory-palace technique works, and why trained volunteers using it roughly doubled what they could recall in a 2017 study published in Neuron.

SomnuMatrix takes a dream and gives it back its geography. Instead of a page you never re-read, you get a street you can walk down.

Your dreams stay yours

Your journal is kept on your own device. To build a dream, its words pass through our AI reader and come back as a place — we don't keep them, and nothing you dream reaches the city unless you take it there.

It is not therapy

SomnuMatrix isn't a medical device and makes no treatment claims. It's a journal that builds a world. If dreams are troubling you, talk to someone qualified.

Questions

The things people ask

Is this real, or a concept?

It's built and running. Everything on this page, and everything in the film, is counted or recorded from the working thing.

Do I have to write a lot?

No. A sentence builds something. The more you write, the more of the dream is there — but a line at four in the morning is enough.

Is AI involved?

Yes. An AI reader goes through every dream you write so it can build exactly what you saw, and it gives the people you dreamt a voice. It doesn't invent things you didn't dream.

What happens to my dreams?

Your journal stays on your device. The words go through the AI reader to be built and aren't kept by us. The shared city only ever sees what you choose to bring into it.

Is it a game?

Your dreamscape isn't — there's nothing to win; it's a journal you can walk in. The city is more like a living world: work, money, homes, shops and courts, run by the people in it.

Which platforms?

It runs in your browser, on a computer or a phone. Nothing to install.

Do I pay now?

No. The list is free and nothing is charged. You'll get word the day it opens, with the launch price.
